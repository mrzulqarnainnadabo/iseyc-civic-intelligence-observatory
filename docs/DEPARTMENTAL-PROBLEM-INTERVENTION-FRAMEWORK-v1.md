# Departmental Problem-to-Intervention Framework v1

**Status date:** 2026-11-14  
**Branch:** feat/national-evidence-reconciliation-v1  
**Purpose:** Connect national evidence to realistic ISEYC departmental action. Not a claim of national programme coverage or solved problems.

## Common record schema

For every problem-intervention record:

1. Problem statement  
2. Supporting evidence (with source, period, geography)  
3. Geographic coverage  
4. Affected population (only if reliable data supports it)  
5. Responsible government institution  
6. Evidence limitations  
7. What ISEYC can realistically contribute  
8. Proposed intervention  
9. Required expertise and partners  
10. Resources required  
11. Implementation risks  
12. Expected measurable outputs  
13. Expected outcomes  
14. Verification method  
15. Reporting period  

**Responsibility split (always explicit)**  
- Government responsibility  
- ISEYC responsibility  
- Partner responsibility  
- Community participation  

## Prioritisation criteria (transparent, not a ranking engine)

- Evidence quality  
- Severity of documented problem  
- Geographic coverage of evidence  
- Potential public value  
- Feasibility given ISEYC capacity  
- Cost and resources  
- Safeguarding and implementation risk  
- Ability to measure results  

Where evidence is insufficient → recommend further research. Do not invent priority scores.

## A. Education and Capacity Building

### Evidence domains to track
- School enrolment / attendance / out-of-school children  
- Literacy and learning outcomes  
- Teacher availability and school infrastructure  
- Digital literacy (distinguish programme counts from population prevalence)  
- Youth employment and vocational skills  
- Higher education to employment transition  

### Example problem → intervention (illustrative structure)

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| Problem | Large numbers of young people lack practical digital skills needed for local work and civic participation. |
| Supporting evidence | NITDA DL4ALL dashboard counts; NBS / UNESCO literacy indicators where available; limitations must be retained. |
| Geographic coverage | National programme claims vs actual community reach must be distinguished. |
| Responsible institution | Federal Ministry of Education; State ministries; NITDA; SUBEBs. |
| ISEYC contribution | Community-level digital literacy and vocational workshops with pre/post competency checks. |
| Intervention | Time-bound digital literacy cohorts; vocational short courses tied to local demand. |
| Outputs | Enrolment, completion, competency assessment scores. |
| Outcomes | Demonstrated skill use (portfolio, placement, or documented application). |
| Verification | Attendance registers, assessment instruments, sample follow-up — not self-reported national prevalence. |
| Risks | Over-claiming population impact; conflating programme records with unique beneficiaries. |

**Do not:** Treat national education statistics as a description of every community.  
**Measure:** Competency improvement, completion, demonstrated skills.

## B. Health and Well-being

### Evidence domains
- Primary healthcare availability and facility distribution  
- Maternal and child health, immunisation, nutrition  
- Health workforce  
- Essential medicines (only where reliable data exists)  
- Preventive health and mental well-being  
- Geographic accessibility  

### ISEYC role boundary
- Community health literacy and awareness  
- Maternal/child health education  
- Referral and navigation support  
- Partnerships with qualified providers  

**Do not:** Present awareness campaigns as clinical services or make medical claims beyond qualified guidance.  
**Measure:** Verified programme reach, referrals completed, demonstrated knowledge gains.

NPHCDA BHCPF and facility-upgrade figures in the Observatory are publisher-supported national administrative counts — not facility-level functionality proof for every LGA.

## C. Software and Technology

### Evidence domains
- Internet and telecoms access  
- Digital skills and technology adoption  
- Digital public services  
- Local technology capacity and digital learning infrastructure  

### ISEYC role
- Practical digital literacy and software skills training  
- Civic technology prototypes solving identified local problems  
- Community information systems with clear ownership  

**Do not:** Build tools without an identifiable user need and maintenance path.  
**Measure:** Adoption, functionality, reliability, demonstrated usefulness — not download counts alone.

Distinguish: subscriptions ≠ unique users; programme targets ≠ measured population outcomes.

## D. Civic Engagement and Accountability

### Evidence domains
- Electoral participation and INEC data availability (see INEC coverage matrix)  
- Public budgets and expenditure evidence  
- Institutional responsibilities  
- Public information accessibility  
- Community demands (Civic Mandate published records)  
- Transparency mechanisms  

### ISEYC role
- Electoral and budget literacy  
- Community evidence documentation  
- Citizen understanding of public institutions  
- Evidence-based civic engagement  

**Non-partisan rule:** No party ranking, candidate endorsement, or unsupported political claims.  
**Measure:** Documented participation quality, evidence literacy, traceable institutional responses (not “awareness” alone).

INEC matrix makes clear: registered voters are available for all 37 jurisdictions (2023); accredited voters and state-level valid/rejected matrices are **not** yet extracted into Observatory observations.

---

## Cross-department rule

When a question crosses departments, explain relationships without mixing incompatible measurements (e.g. health expenditure share of budget is not a measure of facility functionality or health outcomes).
