# Nigeria: What the Published Evidence Currently Shows

**National presentation working brief**  
**Evidence cut-off:** 12 November 2026  
**Status:** Human-review draft. This is a source-attributed evidence snapshot, not a complete national performance assessment.

## Executive summary

The current evidence points to three important realities:

1. **State budgets are not translating into reported spending at the same pace.** BudgIT reports that 34 states had combined approved budgets of ₦35.22 trillion but spent ₦8.60 trillion by the end of Q2 2026 — 24.4% of the combined budgets. The arithmetic gap is ₦26.62 trillion, calculated from those two reported figures; it is not a separate audited balance.
2. **Education and health spending vary sharply between states.** BudgIT reports ₦1.049 trillion in education expenditure across 34 states. Its later detailed H1 health report reports ₦621.34 billion in health expenditure across 34 states; an earlier infographic reported ₦614.55 billion, a difference that remains under reconciliation. State shares and amounts vary; these are expenditure records, not proof of school or facility outcomes.
3. **Public fiscal information is uneven.** BudgIT's Q2 2026 fiscal transparency table scores all 36 states. Eight score 100, while Rivers scores 15. The score measures the availability of citizens' budgets, quarterly BIRs, e-procurement and fiscal-data websites—not the quality of public services.

The Observatory has located 15 official state budget portals and three additional official state budget documents in its source-discovery pass. Eighteen states remain unreviewed in that pass. The all-state fiscal transparency table provides a separate 36-state disclosure view. Neither should be presented as a complete 36-state budget execution dataset.

## Slide 1 — State budgets: appropriation versus expenditure

**H1 2026, 34 states:**
- Combined approved budgets: **₦35.22tn**
- Actual expenditure by end-Q2: **₦8.60tn**
- Expenditure performance: **24.4%**
- Calculated difference: **₦26.62tn**

State-level expenditure performance reported by BudgIT:
- Oyo: 43.4%
- Bauchi: 42.6%
- Ekiti: 37.9%
- Enugu: 10.5%
- Cross River: 11.1%

**Source:** [BudgIT — H1 2026 Total Expenditure Performance by State](https://budgit.org/post_infographics/h1-2026-total-expenditure-performance-by-state/)

**What this means:** The first-half outturn is materially below the combined approved budget in the 34-state sample. The source page does not name the two states excluded. Do not assume the sample is the same as the revenue or health review.

**Do not claim:** The unspent difference is cash in government accounts, funds withheld, abandoned projects or money lost. The source does not establish those conclusions.

## Slide 2 — Education: budget execution and state variation

BudgIT reports **₦1.049tn** in combined education expenditure across 34 states by end-Q2 2026.

Selected state indicators:
- Education share of total expenditure: Jigawa 29.9%, Ekiti 25.1%, Nasarawa 24.9%.
- Lower reported shares: Akwa Ibom 0.6%, Imo 4.2%, Lagos 5.8%.
- Highest absolute spending: Kano ₦99.23bn, Lagos ₦88.96bn, Delta ₦65.50bn.

**Source:** [BudgIT — H1 2026 Education Expenditure Performance by State](https://budgit.org/post_infographics/h1-2026-education-expenditure-performance-by-state/)

**Department interpretation:** Compare expenditure with school census reporting completeness, teacher deployment, classroom condition, sanitation, learning materials, assessment results and school-level verification. A higher share of expenditure does not automatically mean better learning.

## Slide 3 — Health: budget execution and service verification

BudgIT's later detailed H1 2026 report reports **₦621.34bn** in combined health expenditure across 34 states by end-Q2 2026. Rivers and Osun were excluded because their Q2 BIRs were unavailable. The earlier 21 October infographic reported ₦614.55bn; the difference is not explained in the public page summaries and remains a high-priority reconciliation issue.

Selected state indicators:
- Health share of total expenditure: Kano 19.7%, Kebbi 12.5%, Jigawa 11.6%.
- Lower reported shares: Akwa Ibom approximately 0.1%, Benue 0.7%, Cross River 1.4%.
- Highest absolute spending: Lagos ₦119.51bn, Kano ₦79.17bn, Bauchi ₦42.03bn.

**Current source:** [BudgIT — Nigeria’s Subnational Health Expenditure Report, H1 2026](https://budgit.org/post_publications/nigerias-subnational-health-expenditure-report-h1-2026/)  
**Earlier source:** [BudgIT — H1 2026 Health Expenditure Performance by State](https://budgit.org/post_infographics/h1-2026-health-expenditure-performance-by-state/)

The detailed report also states that health budget performance declined from **44.95% in H1 2025 to 40.48% in H1 2026**, despite a **46.17% nominal increase** in approved health budgets; actual health expenditure increased by **31.61% nominally**. These are secondary aggregates for the 34-state comparison, not inflation-adjusted service outcomes.

**Department interpretation:** Reconcile state BIRs with facility-level NHFR records, staffing, opening hours, medicine stock, NHLMIS consumption, BHCPF disbursement and PHC functionality. Spending is not proof that a medicine reached a facility or a PHC is operational.

## Slide 4 — Public fiscal information: all 36 states

BudgIT's Q2 2026 States Fiscal Transparency League scores all 36 states on:
- Citizens' budget availability: 15 points
- Quarterly BIR availability: 16 points
- E-procurement portal: 11 points
- Fiscal-data website: 12 points
- Normalised total: 100%

Key findings:
- Eight states score 100: Adamawa, Benue, Ebonyi, Ekiti, Gombe, Jigawa, Kaduna and Kebbi.
- Sixteen states score 90 or above.
- Rivers scores 15; Nasarawa 57; Osun 67.
- Kano, Niger and Rivers score zero for e-procurement.
- Osun and Rivers score zero for quarterly BIR availability.
- Nasarawa and Rivers score zero for citizens' budget availability.

**Source:** [BudgIT States Fiscal Transparency League — Q2 2026](https://fiscalleague.budgit.org/latest-ranking)

**Civic Department interpretation:** Treat these as gaps in public fiscal information. Prioritise source acquisition, accessible citizens' budgets, complete BIRs, functioning e-procurement records and public audit trails. Do not present the score as a measure of corruption, public trust or service delivery.

## Slide 4A — 2025 state fiscal performance

Agora Policy's review of 35 states' 2025 Budget Implementation Reports (Rivers excluded) reports:
- Combined revenue: **₦16.64tn**; combined expenditure: **₦19.21tn**.
- Total budget implementation: **68.2%**; capital expenditure: **₦11.87tn**; recurrent expenditure: **₦7.34tn**.
- Capital implementation: **62.9%**; recurrent implementation: **79%**.
- FAAC supplied approximately **75%** of actual revenue and IGR **25%**; 27 states did not meet projected revenue targets.

Selected state comparisons:
- Highest total budget execution: Yobe 99.8%, Delta 97.2%, Ekiti 87.9%.
- Lowest total budget execution: Taraba 34.0%, Kebbi 41.3%, Abia 45.5%, Niger 47.1%, Plateau 47.5%, Cross River 48.9%.
- Highest actual revenue: Lagos ₦2,627.9bn, Delta ₦1,449.4bn, Akwa Ibom ₦1,113.2bn.
- Highest IGR share of revenue: Lagos 70%, Enugu 61%, Ogun 54%; lowest: Bayelsa 5%, Yobe 6%, Sokoto 7%.

The Observatory now provides an interactive 35-state table for actual revenue, actual expenditure, total budget execution, capital execution, IGR share and IGR budget performance. Rivers is marked unavailable, not zero.

**Sources:** [Agora Policy — Review of Performance of States' Budgets in 2025](https://agorapolicy.org/our-blogs/251-review-of-performance-of-states-budgets-in-2025.html) · [BudgIT — 2026 State of States Report](https://budgit.org/post_publications/from-allocation-to-impact-rethinking-state-spending-priorities-2026-state-of-states-report/)

BudgIT reports Lagos 2025 IGR of **₦1.71tn** and Enugu IGR of **₦407.76bn**, up 125.91% nominally from 2024. Agora Policy cites **₦1.85tn** for Lagos IGR. The **₦140bn difference is unreconciled**; keep both source-attributed and do not average or present either as definitive without primary audited accounts.

**Interpretation boundary:** Secondary analysis of 35 state BIRs; not a 36-state audited dataset. A fiscal execution rate does not prove project completion or service quality.

## Slide 5 — Federal fiscal context

The State House reports that the 2026 federal appropriation is **₦68.32tn**, comprising:
- Capital expenditure: ₦32.2tn
- Recurrent expenditure: ₦15.4tn
- Debt service: ₦15.8tn
- Statutory transfers: ₦4.799tn

**Source:** [State House — 2026 Appropriation assent, 17 April 2026](https://statehouse.gov.ng/president-tinubu-assents-to-2026-appropriation-bill-and-2025-budget-extension/)

The 2025 federal budget implementation period was subsequently extended to **31 December 2026**. This means interim 2025 federal outturns should not be described as final before that cut-off. [State House extension notice](https://statehouse.gov.ng/president-tinubu-signs-appropriation-amendment-no-4-bill-2025-extends-implementation-to-december-31-2026/).

**Source:** [State House — 2025 budget implementation extension, 30 September 2026](https://statehouse.gov.ng/president-tinubu-signs-appropriation-amendment-no-4-bill-2025-extends-implementation-to-december-31-2026/)

**Interpretation boundary:** Federal appropriation is not state appropriation, cash release, actual expenditure or verified delivery. The 2025 extension must be considered before treating federal 2025 outturn as final.

## Slide 6 — Technology: distinguish reach from outcomes

NITDA's Digital Literacy for All dashboard, updated 24 October 2026, reports:
- **2,783,731** verified programme beneficiaries since January 2022.
- **5.57%** progress against the 50 million beneficiary target.
- **14,747 communities** and **774 LGAs** reached; dashboard reports coverage across 36 states and the FCT.
- **33% female participation** and 2,100,027 beneficiaries under age 35.

**Source:** [NITDA Digital Literacy for All dashboard](https://dashboard.digitalliteracy.nitda.gov.ng/)

These are programme-reported reach figures, not independently verified digital competence, sustained employment, device access or service adoption. The dashboard's national coverage does not provide state-level beneficiary or outcome breakdowns in this snapshot.

**Department interpretation:** Ask for state/LGA-level participant registers, completion and assessment results, competency tests, certification, employment outcomes, connectivity and device access, programme costs and independent verification. Do not turn national programme reach into a state-level digital skills estimate.

## Slide 6A — Anambra: nine-month fiscal outturn from an official Q3 report

Anambra's official Q3 2026 Budget Implementation Report covers **1 January to 30 September 2026** and was published on **28 October 2026**. The state reports:
- Total revenue including opening balance: **₦300.80bn**, or **39.3%** of the ₦766.37bn original budget.
- Total expenditure: **₦229.80bn**, or **30.0%** of the original budget.
- Capital expenditure: **₦165.76bn**, or **27.8%** of the ₦596.77bn capital budget.
- Independent revenue: **₦50.09bn**, or **69.6%** of the ₦72.00bn budget.

**Primary source:** [Anambra Government — Open Finance, Q3 2026 BIR](https://smart.anambrastate.gov.ng/open-finance) (Table 1, page 8 of the report; prepared by the Office of the Accountant General, Ministry of Finance and Ministry of Budget and Economic Planning).

**What this means:** The report's own nine-month elapsed-time yardstick is 75.0%. Total and capital expenditure ratios are below that yardstick, while independent revenue is closer to its annual budget. This is a fiscal execution comparison only.

**Do not claim:** That the difference between budget and expenditure is cash available, lost funds, abandoned projects or proof of service failure. The report does not by itself establish whether roads, schools, PHCs or other assets were completed and are functioning.

**Civic Department follow-up:** Reconcile Q1–Q3 BIRs, 2026 revised budget/virements, procurement awards, payment records, contract milestones and physical verification. Sector departments should use the PHC and basic-education sections in the same BIR only after those tables are separately extracted and checked.

## Publication rules

- Every number must display source, period, unit, geography and coverage denominator.
- Keep official data, published research and media reporting visibly distinct.
- Budget allocation, release, expenditure, procurement, completion and functionality are separate states.
- Do not infer that money was lost or that a service improved from a budget figure alone.
- Do not rank states across incomparable data or missing reporting periods.
- No blank or unavailable figure becomes zero.
- Human review is required before any national presentation or public claim.
