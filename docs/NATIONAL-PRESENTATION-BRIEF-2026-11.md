# Nigeria: What the Published Evidence Currently Shows

**National presentation working brief**  
**Evidence cut-off:** 12 November 2026  
**Status:** Human-review draft. This is a source-attributed evidence snapshot, not a complete national performance assessment.

## Executive summary

The current evidence points to three important realities:

1. **State budgets are not translating into reported spending at the same pace.** BudgIT reports that 34 states had combined approved budgets of ₦35.22 trillion but spent ₦8.60 trillion by the end of Q2 2026 — 24.4% of the combined budgets. The arithmetic gap is ₦26.62 trillion, calculated from those two reported figures; it is not a separate audited balance.
2. **Education and health spending vary sharply between states.** BudgIT reports ₦1.049 trillion in education expenditure and ₦614.55 billion in health expenditure across 34 states by end-Q2 2026. State shares and amounts vary; these are expenditure records, not proof of school or facility outcomes.
3. **Public fiscal information is uneven.** BudgIT's Q2 2026 fiscal transparency table scores all 36 states. Eight score 100, while Rivers scores 15. The score measures the availability of citizens' budgets, quarterly BIRs, e-procurement and fiscal-data websites—not the quality of public services.

The Observatory has located 13 official state budget portals in its own source-discovery pass. Twenty-three states remain unreviewed in that pass. The all-state fiscal transparency table provides a separate 36-state disclosure view. Neither should be presented as a complete 36-state budget execution dataset.

## Slide 1 — State budgets: appropriation versus expenditure

**H1 2026, 34 states:**
- Combined approved budgets: **₦35.22tn**
- Actual expenditure by end-Q2: **₦8.60tn**
- Expenditure performance: **24.4%**
- Calculated difference: **₦26.62tn**

State-level expenditure performance reported by BudgIT:
- Oyo: 43.4%
- Bauchi: 42.6%
- Ekiti: 37.9%
- Enugu: 10.5%
- Cross River: 11.1%

**Source:** [BudgIT — H1 2026 Total Expenditure Performance by State](https://budgit.org/post_infographics/h1-2026-total-expenditure-performance-by-state/)

**What this means:** The first-half outturn is materially below the combined approved budget in the 34-state sample. The source page does not name the two states excluded. Do not assume the sample is the same as the revenue or health review.

**Do not claim:** The unspent difference is cash in government accounts, funds withheld, abandoned projects or money lost. The source does not establish those conclusions.

## Slide 2 — Education: budget execution and state variation

BudgIT reports **₦1.049tn** in combined education expenditure across 34 states by end-Q2 2026.

Selected state indicators:
- Education share of total expenditure: Jigawa 29.9%, Ekiti 25.1%, Nasarawa 24.9%.
- Lower reported shares: Akwa Ibom 0.6%, Imo 4.2%, Lagos 5.8%.
- Highest absolute spending: Kano ₦99.23bn, Lagos ₦88.96bn, Delta ₦65.50bn.

**Source:** [BudgIT — H1 2026 Education Expenditure Performance by State](https://budgit.org/post_infographics/h1-2026-education-expenditure-performance-by-state/)

**Department interpretation:** Compare expenditure with school census reporting completeness, teacher deployment, classroom condition, sanitation, learning materials, assessment results and school-level verification. A higher share of expenditure does not automatically mean better learning.

## Slide 3 — Health: budget execution and service verification

BudgIT reports **₦614.55bn** in combined health expenditure across 34 states by end-Q2 2026. Rivers and Osun were excluded because their Q2 BIRs were unavailable.

Selected state indicators:
- Health share of total expenditure: Kano 19.7%, Kebbi 12.5%, Jigawa 11.6%.
- Lower reported shares: Akwa Ibom approximately 0.1%, Benue 0.7%, Cross River 1.4%.
- Highest absolute spending: Lagos ₦119.51bn, Kano ₦79.17bn, Bauchi ₦42.03bn.

**Source:** [BudgIT — H1 2026 Health Expenditure Performance by State](https://budgit.org/post_infographics/h1-2026-health-expenditure-performance-by-state/)

**Department interpretation:** Reconcile state BIRs with facility-level NHFR records, staffing, opening hours, medicine stock, NHLMIS consumption, BHCPF disbursement and PHC functionality. Spending is not proof that a medicine reached a facility or a PHC is operational.

## Slide 4 — Public fiscal information: all 36 states

BudgIT's Q2 2026 States Fiscal Transparency League scores all 36 states on:
- Citizens' budget availability: 15 points
- Quarterly BIR availability: 16 points
- E-procurement portal: 11 points
- Fiscal-data website: 12 points
- Normalised total: 100%

Key findings:
- Eight states score 100: Adamawa, Benue, Ebonyi, Ekiti, Gombe, Jigawa, Kaduna and Kebbi.
- Sixteen states score 90 or above.
- Rivers scores 15; Nasarawa 57; Osun 67.
- Kano, Niger and Rivers score zero for e-procurement.
- Osun and Rivers score zero for quarterly BIR availability.
- Nasarawa and Rivers score zero for citizens' budget availability.

**Source:** [BudgIT States Fiscal Transparency League — Q2 2026](https://fiscalleague.budgit.org/latest-ranking)

**Civic Department interpretation:** Treat these as gaps in public fiscal information. Prioritise source acquisition, accessible citizens' budgets, complete BIRs, functioning e-procurement records and public audit trails. Do not present the score as a measure of corruption, public trust or service delivery.

## Slide 5 — Federal fiscal context

The State House reports that the 2026 federal appropriation is **₦68.32tn**, comprising:
- Capital expenditure: ₦32.2tn
- Recurrent expenditure: ₦15.4tn
- Debt service: ₦15.8tn
- Statutory transfers: ₦4.799tn

**Source:** [State House — 2026 Appropriation assent, 17 April 2026](https://statehouse.gov.ng/president-tinubu-assents-to-2026-appropriation-bill-and-2025-budget-extension/)

The 2025 federal budget implementation period was subsequently extended to **31 December 2026**.

**Source:** [State House — 2025 budget implementation extension, 30 September 2026](https://statehouse.gov.ng/president-tinubu-signs-appropriation-amendment-no-4-bill-2025-extends-implementation-to-december-31-2026/)

**Interpretation boundary:** Federal appropriation is not state appropriation, cash release, actual expenditure or verified delivery. The 2025 extension must be considered before treating federal 2025 outturn as final.

## Slide 6 — Technology: distinguish reach from outcomes

NITDA's Digital Literacy for All programme reports national reach and programme progress in its dashboard. Those figures describe programme-reported reach, not independently verified digital competence, sustained employment, device access or service adoption.

**Department interpretation:** Ask for state/LGA-level participant registers, completion and assessment results, competency tests, certification, employment outcomes, connectivity and device access, programme costs and independent verification. Do not turn national programme reach into a state-level digital skills estimate.

## Slide 7 — What is still unknown

- The current portal-discovery register has official budget portals for 13 states; 23 states remain unreviewed in this pass.
- The NGF Public Finance Database describes all-36-state budget and audited-account coverage, but its landing page rendered unrelated injected third-party links. No numeric values have been ingested from it.
- BudgIT's 2025 State of States report covers 35 states; detailed state-level extraction and methodology review remain pending.
- BudgIT's H1 2026 total-expenditure and education pages cover 34 states but do not identify the excluded states in their page text.
- The FCT is tracked separately; its final enacted 2026 appropriation law and official implementation reports were not confirmed in this source review.
- The Budget Office lists a Q2 2026 federal BIR, published 7 November 2026, but its Download and View links redirect to a login page. No values have been extracted from that report.

## Publication rules

- Every number must display source, period, unit, geography and coverage denominator.
- Keep official data, published research and media reporting visibly distinct.
- Budget allocation, release, expenditure, procurement, completion and functionality are separate states.
- Do not infer that money was lost or that a service improved from a budget figure alone.
- Do not rank states across incomparable data or missing reporting periods.
- No blank or unavailable figure becomes zero.
- Human review is required before any national presentation or public claim.
